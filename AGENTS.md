## Reusable video system across conversations

For video-system development and new video production, start at `video-system/RESUME.md` and run `node video-system/status.mjs --project=nhat-thanh` from the workspace root. The system is the deliverable; episode2 is a test case. Read the selected project profile and its actual workflow cursor; do not rebuild context from chat history or assume an old preview is approved. Keep reusable operations independent of episode-specific content and character identity. User preference: productivity first, understandable story/action and continuity next; rough pose cuts are acceptable. Do not impose continuous biomechanics or fine polish as default requirements. Reuse the catalog thoroughly; generate only for specific unresolved needs. Preserve the separate native render authorization and honest scoring rules below when they apply.

For any work inside `motion_intro/`:

1. For routine preview authoring, resume from `motion_intro/renders/work/<EP>/<SCENE>/scene-capsule.json` and route through `motion_intro/AGENT-INDEX.md`; do not dump the full episode manifest or full policy documents into context. Read `quality-constitution.md` and `series-bible.md` in full before native rendering, QG-2 scoring, approval, release, completion reporting, or when the capsule/index explicitly routes to a policy section.
2. Creative choices are free unless the user fixes a direction. Do not prescribe a mandatory lens, transition, palette, layout, or animation technique merely to satisfy a checklist.
3. Scoring is not free. Apply QG-2 defect caps to the rendered video. Never score intent, code, JSON, or an MD plan as if it were visible execution.
4. Never claim a scene or episode is above 8 unless the QG-2 validator passes, both review passes say `pass`, no open defect remains, and the evidence includes a playable render plus 2 fps shot boards.
5. A visible defect must lower the related category even when the story is clear or technical delivery is clean. Technical QC cannot compensate for weak direction, performance, depth, or style.
6. If a new render contradicts an earlier score, invalidate the earlier approval, record the defect, reset the scene to `redo-required`, and report the lower honest score.
7. Do not render or call a master final while one scene is `redo-required`. A preview, plan, storyboard, or successful render command is not completion.
8. On every new conversation, treat `series/episodes/<EP>/episode.json` as the source workflow cursor but read it through `scene:context`/targeted extraction: report its `revision`, `previewWorkflow.activeScene`, and `workflow.activeScene` before acting. Preserve the contiguous `approved-by-user` prefix. A change confined to the active or a later scene must not invalidate earlier locked scenes merely because they share one renderer source file; invalidate an approved scene only when that scene's locked render or effective visual/audio/timing/asset inputs changed.
9. Before any scene render, use the active Remotion composition in Preview Studio to inspect playback and authored frame ranges. Fix visible blocking defects in preview first; never render merely to discover position, motion, continuity or flicker problems. Native scene rendering also requires explicit user authorization in the current conversation; a passing Preview Studio check is not permission to render. Preview Studio is a preflight tool and never substitutes for native QG-2 evidence or official scoring.
10. Project completion target is QG-2 9+: every rubric must be at least 9.0 and the arithmetic overall must be strictly above 9.0. An agent may not stop and report a scene or episode as complete until rendered evidence, both review passes, full-speed playback, validator success and zero open defects prove this target. Every completion report must include the full ten-category score table, overall score, lowest category, evidence paths and defect status. If evidence, render authorization or the 9+ target is missing, report progress or `redo-required`—never completion and never invented scores.
11. Before a long tool call and after every completed/failed workflow step, update the scene capsule. After context compaction, read only the capsule first and execute its `resumeCommand`; do not reconstruct state by replaying chat or rereading whole JSON/log files. Use `scene:patch` for scene metadata changes so a preview-active scene cannot accidentally mutate another scene.

## Creative memory for video work

For video creative work, read `creative-memory/RESUME.md` and its linked style handbook before proposing or changing the visual treatment. Preserve the existing episode/capsule workflow where applicable. When the user praises a shot or creative choice, append the exact feedback, scoped subject, date, available evidence/version and limitations to `creative-memory/feedback.json`; update the handbook when it reveals a reusable preference. Do not infer official approval or a numeric quality score from praise. Treat examples as contextual references, not mandatory creative recipes.
